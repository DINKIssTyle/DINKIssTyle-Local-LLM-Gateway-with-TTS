# Source selection

Source quality review: Before selecting, reason privately in this order. (1) Identify the specific information missing from already_heard that the task needs. (2) Compare the candidates: distinguish concrete factual explanation, attributed observation, opinion, personal reflection and persuasion; assess what the supplied source can actually support. A URL or title is context, not proof of authority; do not invent credentials or unseen evidence. Do not accept or reject a passage merely because it is a blog, a vendor page or an official-looking site. Technical blogs can explain mechanisms; vendors can document specifications, but their self-praise does not establish superiority. (3) Challenge the tentative choice: what useful, specific information would the wearer learn, and is it supported here rather than inferred from topic overlap, promotional confidence or eloquence? Reject advertising, purchase appeals, unsupported superlatives, essay-like reflection and generic advice when they do not answer the information need. Opinions or personal experiences are eligible only when the task calls for them and the selected text preserves their attribution, scope and limitations. Do not require numbers or citations in every useful explanation. (4) Select the smallest sufficient set of sentences allowed by the output contract, preserving necessary conditions and qualifications. Omit surrounding sales or reflective prose; if it cannot be separated without changing the claim, skip. Recheck that the result directly answers the task and adds information. If no candidate survives, use the contract's skip result rather than the least bad candidate. Keep this deliberation private; return only the enclosing task's required output, with no reasoning or extra fields.

Saved-memory records support personal-history tasks only; they are not independent verification of public facts. Retrieval headers, scores, translation settings, language notices, tool status and assistant instructions do not answer factual tasks. If candidates contain only those, skip even if their topic overlaps.

Translation catalogs and bilingual example sentences illustrate language usage; they do not establish the truth of their example claims. Unless the task specifically asks for translation or usage, reject "Translations in context of ..." records and similar language-example pages. A complete example sentence about plants, history or technology is still not evidence about that subject. If all candidates are wording matches instead of substantive answers, skip.
